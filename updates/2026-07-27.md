## Cinematic dubbing and production lip-sync lineage

Three recent systems now expose their data strategy as navigable model ↔
dataset relations instead of leaving dataset names inside paper prose:

- **DiFlowDubber** pretrains a zero-shot TTS prior on a disclosed 470-hour
  LibriTTS subset, then adapts separate models on Chem and GRID. LibriTTS and
  GRID have official download surfaces; Chem does not. The official repository
  remains a paper/project shell and still marks implementation and checkpoints
  as coming soon.
- **FunCineForge** trains its main 0.5B cinematic-dubbing system on CineDub-CN,
  a 7.2 TB internal corpus distilled from more than 6,000 raw Chinese television
  hours into 1,559,172 samples and more than 4,700 effective speech hours. The
  code and bilingual checkpoint are public, but the full corpus is not. The
  separately released CineDub-Example repository is gated, non-commercial, and
  contains roughly 52.93 GB of Chinese and English examples; it is not presented
  as the complete training set. A controlled comparison links the model to
  V2C-Animation, Chem, and GRID for both training and evaluation roles.
- **UniSync** fine-tunes an undisclosed audio-image-to-video base model on a
  named but unreleased 5,000-video high-resolution corpus. It evaluates on HDTF
  and the new 495-video RealWorld-LipSync benchmark. The paper promises the new
  benchmark for future public release, but no files, repository, weights, or
  license are currently available.

The acquisition layer keeps four states separate: direct official downloads
(LibriTTS and GRID), reconstruction metadata without redistributable media
(V2C-Animation), gated examples (CineDub-Example), project/metadata-only
corpora (Chem and CineDub-CN), and paper-only unavailable corpora (UniSync 5K
and RealWorld-LipSync). Eleven new critical probes watch every added model and
dataset surface for repository, checkpoint, file, access, or license changes.

The resulting catalog contains 99 datasets, 85 models, 115 explicit
model-to-dataset role relations, 26 dataset-lineage relations, and 278 watched
first-party sources. The accepted 278-source live scan completed with zero new

## Video Try-On data-strategy expansion

The Try-On track now includes two complementary 2026 systems and three data
cards that make the difference between a named corpus and a downloadable
release explicit.

- **OmniTryOn** starts from Video-As-Prompt / Wan2.1-I2V-14B and fine-tunes
  exclusively on TryAny-Bench. The public benchmark contains 1,460 paired
  reference/target videos (1,243 train and 217 test) retained from more than
  1,500 e-commerce videos. Its two Hugging Face archives are directly hosted,
  but the repository declares no license or dataset card, so access is not
  represented as permission.
- **iTryOn** uses a two-stage recipe: 10K steps on the public ViViD corpus,
  followed by 10K steps on VVT-Interact. VVT-Interact contains 5,292
  video-garment pairs across six interaction categories, with global captions
  and time-stamped action captions. The paper provides no files, request form,
  or release date, so its data card is deliberately marked unavailable rather
  than linking the paper as a download.
- **ViViD** now has its own acquisition card. The official repository hosts a
  roughly 32.1 GB archive covering 9,700 garment-video pairs and 1,213,694
  frames plus agnostic, mask, and DensePose conditions. The repository declares
  Apache-2.0, while the underlying Net-A-Porter media still requires a separate
  upstream-rights review.

Both model cards expose their stage-by-stage operations, inherited foundation
data unknowns, public/unreleased references, and canonical model ↔ dataset
links. Five new critical revision probes watch both model surfaces and all
three datasets.
